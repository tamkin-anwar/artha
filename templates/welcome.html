How Artha works

Every number answers a question you actually have.

Can I afford this? Am I on track this month? Where did it all go? Artha puts the answer first and keeps the spreadsheet out of sight.

Plan

A month that adds up before it starts.

Set one budget and Artha splits it into the bills you have to pay, everyday spending, and what’s left. Category limits are suggested from what you actually spend, not from a template.

  • Bills paid and still to come, in one list
  • Limits per category, only where you want them
  • Over a limit shows in amber: information, not a scolding

Assistant

Ask in plain words. Nothing changes until you say so.

  • Every suggested change shown before it’s saved
  • Your data isn’t used to train AI models

Questions

Does Artha connect to my bank?

No. You add spending yourself, from a statement you review, or from Apple Pay through a shortcut you set up. Artha never asks for a bank login.

How is “safe to spend” worked out?

From your monthly budget, what you’ve already spent, and the bills still due before the month ends, spread over the days left. It’s only as good as what you’ve entered, so the bills list matters.

Is there an app?

Yes. Artha for iPhone, iPad and Mac is in beta on TestFlight, on the same account as the website. Join the beta.

Does the assistant learn from my data?

No. It runs on Anthropic’s API, which doesn’t train on customer data by default, and Artha never opts in. It can suggest changes, but only you can save them.

Is Artha financial advice?

No. It helps you see and plan your own money. For advice about investments, tax or debt, talk to a qualified professional.

Who makes Artha?

Anwar Creative Studio, an independent software company in California that also makes the email app Corres. Write to support@arthaapp.com and a person reads it.
